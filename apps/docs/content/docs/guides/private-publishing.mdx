---
title: Publish a revision
description: Turn a saved Next.js or Laravel revision into a running application on your infrastructure.
---

Publishing makes a selected app revision available as a deployment. It is a separate,
explicit action: building an app or opening a preview does not publish it.

<ExecutionFlow kind="publishing" />

## Before you publish

- You are the **project owner** and have a successful saved revision.
- Your instance operator has completed [publishing setup](/docs/guides/publishing-setup).
- You have reviewed the app in [local preview](/docs/guides/local-previews).

Next.js and Laravel13 are supported. Managed generated-app databases, persistent uploads
and background-worker lifecycles remain separate capabilities.

## Publish from the workspace

1. Open the project and select the revision you want from history.
2. In the Preview toolbar, choose **Publish this version**.
3. Watch its recorded status. You can reload; execution continues independently.
4. When verified, use **Published app** to open the routed application.

A latest successful publish for this revision replaces the redundant Publish action
with access to the published app. Publishing again from another revision is an explicit action.

## Find earlier deployments

Open **Projects → Deployments** to review attempts for projects you can currently read.
**Open revision** returns to the saved source and workspace; it does not publish again.
A historical application link is not a live health check.

## If publishing needs attention

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Publishing unavailable | Ask the instance operator to review publishing configuration. |
| Failed | Review the saved diagnostic; fix the cause, then retry deliberately. Your build and preview are retained. |
| Interrupted or unconfirmed | Ask the operator to verify remote activity and reconcile the saved operation before trying again. |
| Queued | Wait for the executor, or cancel before the operation is claimed. |

Closing the browser does not cancel a deployment. Wrenly does not automatically repeat
an uncertain remote operation. [Operator recovery and limits](/docs/reference/publishing-operations)
explain the next steps for configuration and interrupted work.

## Why publishing keeps the exact image

An image tag can move to different content later. Wrenly verifies the uploaded image's
content hash (its **digest**) and asks the deployer to run that exact image. This avoids
rebuilding generated source with different dependencies or settings on the deployment
server. Registry credentials are used for promotion; application secrets are supplied
at runtime, rather than committed into source or included as build-time credentials.
